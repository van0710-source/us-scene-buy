Unclog a kitchen sink without chemicals

Most grease and food clogs respond better to pressure and a strip snake than to a second mystery bottle — especially in rentals, septic homes, or homes with kids/pets.

  • Start: Cup-style sink plunger (not a toilet flange) for a total stoppage — seal the overflow if your sink has one.
  • Next: A plastic drain snake (Zip-It style) if the opening is large enough and you suspect hair/debris near the top.
  • Skip gels when: you already poured a chemical, you’re on septic, you rent and want lease-safe moves, or pipes are old.
  • Call a pro when: multiple fixtures backup, sewage smell is strong house-wide, or nothing moves after a careful physical try.
Never mix drain chemicals. If something already went down, don’t plunge blindly (splash risk) and don’t stack a second opener.

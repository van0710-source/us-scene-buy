Guide · Dishwasher timing

Kitchen sink smells when the dishwasher runs

That timing is useful evidence. It does not automatically mean “buy stronger drain cleaner.” First separate a local food-residue smell from a hose, trap, or wider drainage problem.

Check in this order

  1. Sink flow: if the sink drains normally, treat odor as the primary symptom—not a full clog.
  2. Disposal area: with power safely off, inspect and clean accessible residue around the rubber splash guard and strainer.
  3. Dishwasher timing: note whether odor appears only during discharge and whether dirty water returns to the sink.
  4. Other fixtures: gurgling, backups, or sewage odor elsewhere point away from another bottle and toward landlord/plumber diagnosis.

Quick decision

Flow normal + local food odor: clean first; buying nothing is reasonable.

Odor + recurring slow grease film: a label-checked maintenance/enzyme lane may fit after cleaning.

Dirty water returns, multiple fixtures react, or sewage odor spreads: stop DIY product shopping.

Never mix drain chemicals. If a chemical opener is already present, follow its label and avoid plunging or adding another product.

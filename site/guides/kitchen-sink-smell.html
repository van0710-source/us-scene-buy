Sink smells bad but water still goes down?

That’s often a cleanup problem before it’s a “buy a clog destroyer” problem — especially if you rent or you’d rather keep harsh chemicals out of the house.

When flow is fine, the smell may be sitting in the strainer, the disposal splash guard, or soft buildup in the near line. Pouring a heavy gel first is how people turn a smell into a chemistry mess.

Try this before you shop

  • Empty and wash the basket strainer.
  • If you have a disposal: clean the rubber splash guard (where gunk loves to hide).
  • Wipe greasy pans into the trash before washing.
  • Hot water after heavy cooking — a habit, not a miracle cure.
Whole-house sewage smell, backups in other fixtures, or water coming up elsewhere → stop DIY shopping and call a plumber or your landlord.

When a product might still help

If odor pairs with a slow drain, or grease film keeps returning, a maintenance-leaning enzyme product (label-checked) can be a better fit than an emergency gel — especially on septic, rentals, or homes with kids/pets.

Guide · Black drain buildup

Black sludge in a kitchen sink drain

Color is less useful than location and flow. Accessible slime around a strainer is a cleaning job; a slow or stopped pipe is a clog decision; repeated backup from deeper in the line is a professional diagnosis.

Match the symptom

  • Visible near the strainer, flow normal: remove accessible residue using the fixture maker’s cleaning guidance. A clog opener is usually unnecessary.
  • Disposal smells or has residue: disconnect power before any accessible cleaning; never reach into the grinding chamber.
  • Slow after greasy cooking: stop sending oil and scraps down the drain, then choose between a physical check and a conservative maintenance lane.
  • Standing water: treat it as a full clog. Do not assume enzyme maintenance will clear it tonight.
  • Returns repeatedly or affects other fixtures: stop buying bottles and contact a landlord or licensed plumber.

What not to do

Do not mix bleach, acids, vinegar, ammonia, or commercial drain openers. Do not plunge over a chemical already in the sink. Do not use appearance alone to identify mold, sewage, or pipe damage.

Primary next step

Normal flow: clean accessible residue and monitor—no purchase required.

Slow flow: use the grease-slow decision path.

No flow or repeat backup: use a physical-first or professional path.

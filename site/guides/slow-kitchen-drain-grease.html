Guide · Slow drain

Slow kitchen drain after cooking? Start here.

If the sink runs fine in the morning and crawls after dinner, you’re often looking at grease and food film — not a mystery that needs the strongest bottle on the shelf.

Cooking oils cool in the line. Tiny layers stack up. Water still moves, just slower. That’s a different problem from a total clog or a sewage smell with normal flow.

Three questions that change the answer

  • Septic or city sewer? Harsh openers are a worse default on septic (and if you’re unsure, stay conservative).
  • Renting, old pipes, kids/pets? Those are brakes on “industrial strength” gels.
  • Already poured a chemical? Don’t stack a second product. Wait, follow the label, or call a plumber.
Never mix drain chemicals. Never promise yourself a guaranteed clear time — labels don’t work that way in real kitchens.

What usually makes sense

  • Grease + caution flags: enzyme / maintenance-leaning products (check the label) or a mechanical try — not panic gels.
  • Fully stuck, city sewer, no flags, nothing tried: a labeled clog gel can be on the table, with plunger/snake still allowed first.
  • Still flowing: strainer + wipe grease to trash often beats shopping.
